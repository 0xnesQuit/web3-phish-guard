# Domain/URL/DNS/registration-layer signals for crypto phishing (fake mint / airdrop / claim pages) vs legitimate projects

Scope note: notes cover only the hostname / URL / DNS / registration / hosting layer and what a browser extension can fetch at page-load time. Content-layer and on-chain signals are out of scope. Sources are 2023-2026 where possible; older sources are flagged. "Signal" (what to look at) is always separated from "evidence" (how reliable it is) and "false-positive examples" (legitimate things that trigger it).

## Key Question 1: Naming patterns (claim-/rewards-/-airdrop/mint-/checker-, hyphenated brand+action) in real campaigns, and which action words dominate

### Takeaway
Brand-plus-action-word combosquatting (e.g. `claim-zama.pages.dev`, `pancakeswapclaim6754.vercel.app`, `rewards-across.events`, `users-ethena.xyz`) is the dominant naming pattern in current crypto drainer campaigns and is visible verbatim in the MetaMask blocklist and in Interisle's Vercel case study, but no public report quantifies the frequency of each action word; the ranking below (claim > airdrop/reward > mint > checker/eligibility) is inferred from blocklist samples, not from a published count.

### Cited Findings
- Interisle's Phishing Landscape 2025 (May 2024-Apr 2025, 1,542,922 unique phishing domains) found 8.9% of phishing domains (137,860) contained a prominent brand name spelled exactly; exact matches for 762 brands were found; "Coinbase" was among the five most frequent brand strings in phishing domains (with USPS, Apple, DHL, Amazon). Interisle explicitly did not do near-match (typo) analysis — [Interisle Phishing Landscape 2025 PDF, p.28](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle: phishers also place brand names in subdomains and URL paths, and many register domains with no brand name or nonsense strings — [Interisle Phishing Landscape 2025 PDF, p.28-29](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle Vercel case study: phishers obtained "hundreds" of vercel.app subdomains to attack PancakeSwap users, e.g. `pancake-swapv2.vercel.app`, `pancakeswapclaim6754.vercel.app`, `v3-pancakeswap.vercel.app` (note the action words "claim", version tokens "v2"/"v3", and hyphen insertion inside the brand) — [Interisle Phishing Landscape 2025 PDF, p.23](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- MetaMask eth-phishing-detect blocklist (live config.json) contains entries such as `rewards-across.events/.info/.network`, `claim-zama.pages.dev`, `claim-hyperliquids.org`, `claims-scroll-io.pages.dev`, `claimzerion-protocol.pages.dev`, `reward-umbraprivacy.com`, `strk.stakingreward.cam`, `vvvchecker.com`, `dumbmoney-checker.sbs`, `airdrop-finder.cyou`, `airdropfinder.bond`, `mintclaimpage402.vercel.app`, `openseamintnft13c.vercel.app`, `claimdogecoin20.github.io`, `claim-your-spot.top`, `claim-it-fast.top`, `claiming-campaign.com`, `claimfeesol.com`, `claimallevm-rewards.top`, `claim.polytoken.us`, `claim.polym2026.xyz` — [MetaMask eth-phishing-detect src/config.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/main/src/config.json)
- Group-IB: Inferno Drainer (Nov 2022-Nov 2023) used 16,000+ unique domains impersonating 100+ crypto brands; lures were "free tokens (airdrops) or rewards for minting NFTs" — [Group-IB press release](https://www.group-ib.com/media-center/press-releases/inferno-drainer); [The Record](https://therecord.media/inferno-drainer-cryptocurrency-scam-spoofing-blockchain-projects)
- Check Point (2025): Inferno Drainer resurfaced; >30,000 victims and >$9M losses in six months — [Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/inferno-drainer-reloaded-deep-dive-into-the-return-of-the-most-sophisticated-crypto-drainer/)
- Fake Chainbase airdrop campaign (PCrisk/CyberInsider): "dozens of lookalike domains" including a `.vip` one, distributed via malicious ads, social spam and DMs; "Connect Wallet" leads to a fake permission prompt or seed-phrase request — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/fake-chainbase-airdrop-sites-drain-crypto-wallets-in-sophisticated-phishing-campaign/)
- PhishDestroy examples (vendor threat-intel pages, snapshots): `airdrop-claim.online`, `airdropsclaim-portal.pages.dev`, `claim-pharos-xyz-eooesft3-asethaf-pe03ser.pages.dev`, `users-ethena.xyz`, `governances-ethena.finance`, `scroll-airdrop.github.io`, `1drop.cyou` ("Airdrop Live | Claim Solana Token Airdrops"), `airdrop.global` (cluster of ~8 domains sharing an infrastructure fingerprint), `trmlabscoin.sbs` (registered 24 Mar 2026 via Gname) — [PhishDestroy airdrop-claim.online](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/airdrop-claim.online/); [PhishDestroy users-ethena.xyz](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/users-ethena.xyz); [PhishDestroy trmlabscoin.sbs](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/trmlabscoin.sbs); [PhishDestroy airdrop.global](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/airdrop.global/)
- Combosquatting (brand + extra word) is far more common than typosquatting in general phishing: Georgia Tech 2017 study found 2.7M combosquatting domains targeting 268 trademarks, ~100x typosquatting; most add a single token; 60% of abusive combosquat domains lived >1,000 days (old source, general web) — [giga.law summary](https://giga.law/blog/2017/12/13/combosquatting); [ICANN list post](https://lists.icann.org/hyperkitty/list/gnso-rpm-wg@icann.org/message/YJZDCRR4IIAXPK2TMDRYBWWL7KZSW4XG/)
- Akamai (2022 phishing dataset, hyphenated domains only): top combosquatting keywords were support, com, login, help, secure, www, account, app, verify, service — no crypto words in the top 10 (general phishing, not crypto-specific) — [Akamai](https://akamai.com/blog/security-research/combosquatting-keyword-analysis-support)
- Fake Arbitrum airdrop wave (March 2023): "hundreds" of fake airdrop sites before the drop; one lookalike impersonated the official airdrop site and asked for fund access — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/beware-of-fake-arbitrum-airdrops-community-warns); [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/community-warns-of-hundreds-of-fake-arbitrum-airdrops/)
- Ethena: official site is ethena.fi; fake "Claim Ethena" sites documented by PCrisk — [PCrisk](https://www.pcrisk.com/removal-guides/29701-claim-ethena-scam)

### Inferences
- Observed action vocabulary in crypto blocklists (ranked by apparent frequency in the MetaMask sample, not a measured count): claim/claims/claiming > reward(s) > airdrop > mint > checker/finder > staking/restake > v2/v3 > eligibility/allocation/presale/bridge/migration. The word "claim" appears both as prefix (`claim-x`), suffix (`x-claim`, `xclaim6754`), and as a subdomain label (`claim.polytoken.us`).
- Scam naming often adds random digits or random suffixes to evade exact blocklists (`mintclaimpage402`, `claiminvites01`, `pancakeswapclaim6754`, `claim-pharos-xyz-eooesft3-...`), so "brand token + action token + digits" is a stronger combination than any single part.
- Hyphenation inside the brand (`pancake-swap`) and brand-token placement in subdomains of free hosts (`*.pages.dev`, `*.vercel.app`, `*.github.io`) are both heavily used; a brand-token-inside-hostname check must run on the full hostname, not only on the registrable domain.
- Combosquatting on legitimate domains is also common (see KQ2), so the pattern alone must never block; it should be a score contributor combined with age, hosting and allowlist checks.

### Gaps
- No ScamSniffer, SlowMist, Chainabuse or academic report found that publishes per-keyword counts ("claim" vs "airdrop" vs "mint") for crypto phishing domains. ScamSniffer annual reports cover losses/victims only (scope: "wallet drainer attacks via phishing websites only") and contain no domain-naming statistics — [ScamSniffer 2025](https://drops.scamsniffer.io/scam-sniffer-2025-crypto-phishing-losses-fall-83-to-84-million/)
- Full MetaMask blocklist not parsed (config.json is ~3 MB; only first 100k chars read). A local script over the full blocklist would give real keyword frequencies.
- The IMC 2025 DaaS paper (32,819 phishing websites) likely has naming/TLD statistics but the ACM PDF returned 403 — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3730567.3764476); [BlockSec summary](https://blocksec.com/blog/inside-ethereum-s-shadow-economy-new-research-unmasks-the-135-m-drainer-as-a-service-industry)

## Key Question 2: How legitimate projects host claim/airdrop/mint flows; legitimate use of "scammy" TLDs; TLD abuse stats

### Takeaway
Legitimate projects use all three patterns (subdomain, subfolder, and entirely separate domains, often on a different TLD than the main brand), and well-known projects live on .xyz/.io/.fi/.link/.foundation/.network, so neither "separate domain" nor "unusual TLD" is safe as a standalone block signal; TLD-level abuse statistics (Interisle, Spamhaus) are strong for .top/.bond/.xin/.cyou/.sbs/.cfd/.lol/.click/.icu and weak or absent for .xyz/.io/.app/.fi/.gg.

### Cited Findings
Legitimate hosting patterns (verified or partially verified):
- EigenLayer: claims "only at claims.eigenfoundation.org" (subdomain of the foundation's domain, which differs from eigenlayer.xyz); Season 1 window 10 May-7 Sep 2024, Season 2 through 16 Mar 2025 — [Eigen Foundation blog](https://blog.eigenfoundation.org/claims-s1-p1/); [Season 2](https://blog.eigenfoundation.org/claims-s2/)
- Starknet: official claim portal provisions.starknet.io (subdomain of main brand), claims 20 Feb-20 Jun 2024; Foundation warned to "follow the correct URL and beware of scams"; program later discontinued — [Starknet blog](https://www.starknet.io/blog/claiming-your-strk/); [Provisions program](https://www.starknet.io/blog/starknet-provisions-program/)
- zkSync: claim site named as claim.zknation.io (separate domain from zksync.io) — secondary source only (KuCoin guide) — [KuCoin](https://kucoin.com/news/articles/zksync-airdrop-guide-how-to-participate-and-claim-zk-tokens)
- LayerZero: ZRO claims opened 20 June 2024 and were to close 20 Sep 2024; claim page named as claim.layerzero.network in low-quality guides only; The Block covers timing — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/301210/arbitrum-record-daily-revenue-layerzero-token-claims); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/layerzero-users-claim-nearly-121-million-from-airdrop-amid-proof-of-donation-controversy/)
- Pudgy Penguins PENGU (Dec 2024): claim links "only shared from the Pudgy Penguins account on X"; CEO told holders to cross-reference Discord and X before connecting — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/pengu-claims-go-live-after-pudgy-penguins-nfts-post-new-all-time-highs)
- Ethena official domain is ethena.fi (a .fi TLD) — [PCrisk](https://www.pcrisk.com/removal-guides/29701-claim-ethena-scam)
- MetaMask's own allowlist documents legitimate brand sprawl across TLDs and across lookalike spellings: `opensea.io / opensea.com / opensea.pro / opensea.foundation`, `walletconnect.com / .org / .network / .foundation`, `reown.com / reown.org`, `magic.link`, `sophon.xyz`, `monadbridge.com`, `magiceden.us`, `pcswap.org`, and deliberately allowlisted lookalikes that are NOT phishing: `metamark.lt`, `metacask.com`, `metacask.io`, `metatask.io`, `metmask.com`, `cryptofitties.com`, `cryptotitties.fun`, `etcerscan.com`, `etherscan.com`, `opengea.org`, `opensea.gr` — [MetaMask config.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/main/src/config.json)
- Legitimate channels get compromised, so "link came from the official account/Discord" is not a safe allow signal: ArbitrumDAO governance X account was hijacked in Feb 2026 to post airdrop-themed phishing links — [MEXC news](https://www.mexc.com/news/626931); a malicious link reached Blast's official Discord during the 2024 airdrop — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/237135/blast-airdrop-scams-verified-gold-check-twitter-users)

TLD abuse statistics:
- Interisle 2025 top TLDs by phishing domains reported: .COM 455,297 (154.7M domains in TLD), .TOP 187,749 (3.5M), .BOND 79,875 (454K), .XYZ 73,509 (4.2M), .SHOP 50,052 (3.3M); TLDs #2-#7 (.TOP .BOND .XYZ .SHOP .INFO .XIN) together have 478,449 phishing domains on ~10% of .COM's base — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.11](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle 2025 phishing score (phishing domains per 10k domains): .XIN 10,810.2 (40K domains, ~100% malicious), .BOND 1,759.0; .COM ~30; new gTLDs are 11% of registrations but 51% of phishing domains; .BOND grew 524%, nearly all via Key-Systems — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.11-12](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf); [Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/2025/09/11/report-names-commonly-used-tlds-for-phishing-attacks/)
- Interisle 5-year "highest phishing score" lists are dominated by ShortDot TLDs (.ICU .BOND .CFD .SBS .CYOU) and XYZ.COM's cheap TLDs (.XYZ .LOL .MONSTER .PICS); .LOL ranked #1 by score in the 2024 study column — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.12-13](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle: 77% of phishing domains were maliciously registered; 37% via bulk registration — [Interisle press release](https://interisle.net/press/pl2025-press-release)
- Spamhaus (Oct 2025-Mar 2026 churn metric): worst TLDs .bond, .lol, .cyou, .sbs, .click, .cfd (secondary report of the Spamhaus update) — [webhosting.today](https://webhosting.today/2026/07/09/which-top-level-domains-are-worth-carrying-the-abuse-data-most-registrars-never-book/); Spamhaus Oct 2024-Mar 2025: .top remained an abuse hotspot — [Spamhaus](https://www.spamhaus.org/resource-hub/domain-reputation/domain-reputation-update-oct-2024-mar-2025/)
- Krebs: phishing up ~40% in year ending Aug 2024, concentrated in cheap new gTLDs .shop, .top, .xyz — [KrebsOnSecurity](https://krebsonsecurity.com/tag/new-gtlds/)
- Halvorson et al. (cited in UCL WEIS 2025): within the first month of registration, new-gTLD domains are more than twice as likely to appear on a blocklist as legacy TLDs — [UCL WEIS 2025 PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10209951/1/CDA_Domains___WEIS_25.pdf)
- IDN homographs are rare in phishing overall: 2,655 IDNs = 0.17% of phishing domains in Interisle's 2024-25 data; example spoof `xn--btpay-b4a.com` (bıtpay.com) — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.29](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- A TLD-risk prior is justified for .top, .bond, .xin, .cyou, .sbs, .cfd, .icu, .click, .lol, .monster, .pics, .shop (Interisle/Spamhaus), but .xyz needs special handling: it is both a top-4 phishing TLD by count and the home of many legitimate crypto projects (sophon.xyz in MetaMask allowlist; Interisle's own .XYZ figure is 73,509 phishing domains out of 4.2M, i.e. ~1.7%). Treat .xyz as a weak positive signal only.
- .io, .fi, .app, .gg, .link, .network, .foundation have no abuse data in the sources found; they should carry zero TLD prior.
- Legitimate "separate claim domain" is common enough (zknation.io, eigenfoundation.org vs eigenlayer.xyz, layerzero.network/.foundation) that "hostname != brand's main domain" cannot be a block signal; it should instead trigger "is this host in a curated allowlist of official claim domains?" and, if not, raise the weight of age/hosting checks.
- Official-channel compromise (Arbitrum X Feb 2026, Blast Discord 2024) means referrer-based allowlisting (KQ6) must not override domain-layer checks.

### Gaps
- Could not verify from primary sources: claim.zknation.io, claim.layerzero.network, arbitrum.foundation airdrop URL, Jupiter (jup.ag) checker, Hyperliquid official domains, Scroll and Blast claim URLs, pump.fun, Magic Eden, Optimism. These should be confirmed from each project's own docs/X before being hard-coded in an allowlist.
- No per-TLD abuse score found for .io/.app/.fi/.gg/.wtf.
- Interisle's full Top-20 TLD tables are at cybercrimeinfocenter.org but were not fetched.

## Key Question 3: Domain age and certificate transparency (RDAP in-browser, CORS, rate limits, crt.sh), typical age of phishing domains, handling brand-new legitimate airdrop sites

### Takeaway
Domain age is the single best-evidenced signal (most phishing domains are used within days of registration and live ~8.6 days on average), and RDAP can be queried from an extension via rdap.org (302 redirect to the authoritative registry; 10 requests/10 s limit) or by consuming IANA's bootstrap file directly, but CORS support is only RECOMMENDED by RFC 7480 and is not guaranteed on every registry; crt.sh is an undocumented, heavily throttled single-instance service unsuitable for per-page-load calls.

### Cited Findings
Age of phishing domains:
- UCL WEIS 2025 (Agarwal & Vasek): 15,126 newly registered phishing domains over 11 months; average lifetime 8.6 days vs 21 hours for an average phishing campaign; 25.7% were previously-registered (re-registered) domains, overwhelmingly originally registered for crime; 26% of resolved domains used Cloudflare reverse proxy; NameSilo was the most abused registrar in their sample while only 2.1% were at Namecheap (contrasting with APWG, which found Namecheap most abused) — [UCL WEIS 2025 PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10209951/1/CDA_Domains___WEIS_25.pdf)
- DNSRF (APWG-based data, ~2022): 63% of phishing domains were less than 4 days old when used; earlier 2017 research found the opposite (phishers aging domains for reputation) — [DNSRF](https://dnsrf.org/blog/phishing-attacks--newly-registered-domains-still-a-prominent-threat)
- Interisle (2025): malicious actors bought at least 10% and possibly ~20% of all new gTLD registrations in 2025 — [Interisle Substack](https://interisle.substack.com/p/malicious-actors-accounted-for-as)
- Interisle Cybercrime Supply Chain 2025: malicious domain registrations +149% YoY, bulk registration for crime +177% — [Interisle](https://interisle.net/cybercrimesupplychain2025)
- Cybercrime Info Center year-over-year (May 2025-Apr 2026): ~7.3M phishing reports and ~2.3M maliciously registered phishing domains (vs ~3.9M / ~1.2M prior year) — [Cybercrime Info Center](https://www.cybercrimeinfocenter.org/phishing-activity-year-over-year-numbers-may-april-2026)
- Sucuri (Angel Drainer, ~4 weeks of URLScan data, early 2024): 5,751 unique domains; 53.6% on CLOUDFLARENET — [Sucuri](https://blog.sucuri.net/2024/02/web3-crypto-malware-angel-drainer.html)
- PhishDestroy flagged `trmlabscoin.sbs` as created 24 Mar 2026 and already serving a drainer (example of same-week use) — [PhishDestroy](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/trmlabscoin.sbs)

RDAP from the browser:
- rdap.org is a bootstrap/redirect service: `https://rdap.org/domain/<name>` answers 302 to the authoritative RDAP server (follow Location) or 404 if no IANA-registered server exists; rate limit "maximum of 10 requests in 10 seconds" (Cloudflare) -> 429; 403 if blocked for abuse; heavy users should consume IANA bootstrap registries directly (RFC 9224); response JSON format is RFC 9083; per-TLD IANA record at `https://rdap.iana.org/domain/{TLD}` — [about.rdap.org](https://about.rdap.org/)
- IANA bootstrap files: `https://data.iana.org/rdap/dns.json` (plus ipv4/ipv6/asn.json), JSON with a `services` array mapping TLDs to base RDAP URLs; clients cache (24 h example) — [rdapper PR](https://github.com/jakejarvis/rdapper/pull/21/files); [RFC 7484bis draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-regext-rfc7484bis-01)
- RFC 7480 only RECOMMENDS `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`; an IETF deployment-findings draft reports that some RDAP servers do not send it and proposes making it MUST for public deployments — [draft-blanchet-regext-rdap-deployfindings](https://ietf.potaroo.net/all-ids/draft-blanchet-regext-rdap-deployfindings-01.html)
- A Dart RDAP client's docs state that IANA, Verisign (.com/.net), PIR (.org) and Google Registry (.dev) send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, while smaller registries without CORS are reachable only from native/server code — [pub.dev rdap](https://pub.dev/documentation/rdap/0.0.2/)
- A browser-based RDAP tool notes that some RDAP servers do not send CORS headers and those queries fail in-page — [xevion.dev](https://xevion.dev/projects/rdap)
- Registration date is in RDAP `events` (eventAction "registration") per RFC 9083 — [about.rdap.org](https://about.rdap.org/) (page points to RFC 9083; field name from the RFC)

Certificate transparency:
- crt.sh has no official API doc, SDK, rate-limit documentation or SLA; it is a single PostgreSQL instance with a Perl CGI front end; JSON via `?q=example.com&output=json`; community-reported throttle ~5 requests/min/IP, with 502s, empty arrays or hangs rather than 429s (vendor-affiliated source, unverified) — [cert.imfht.com rate limit](https://cert.imfht.com/crt-sh-rate-limit); [cert.imfht.com API](https://cert.imfht.com/crt-sh-api)
- UCL: 190 phishing domains in their sample had >50 TLS certificates issued since registration; criminals routinely obtain free certs so "has valid TLS" is meaningless — [UCL WEIS 2025 PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10209951/1/CDA_Domains___WEIS_25.pdf); PhishDestroy pages repeatedly note Let's Encrypt certs on drainer sites — [PhishDestroy airdrop.fight.foundation](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/airdrop.fight.foundation)

### Inferences
- Age thresholds: <7 days = strong positive (covers the 8.6-day average lifetime and the 63%-under-4-days figure); 7-30 days = moderate; >1 year = strong negative weight unless other signals fire. Because 25.7% of phishing NRDs are re-registrations, use the most recent "registration" event, not "first ever seen".
- Extension architecture: at page load, (1) check local allow/block lists, (2) if the registrable domain is unknown, fire one RDAP fetch to `https://rdap.org/domain/<etld+1>` with `redirect: "follow"`; the browser sends Origin "null" after the cross-origin redirect, so success depends on the final registry returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` (true for Verisign/PIR/Google per the Dart docs; untested for .xyz, .top, .io, .fi). Cache results (hours-days) keyed by etld+1 to stay under 10 req/10 s. Free-host subdomains (pages.dev, vercel.app, github.io) must skip RDAP (etld+1 is the provider's) and use the subdomain-provider signal instead.
- A Chrome MV3 extension's service worker fetch is not subject to CORS in the same way as page scripts when the host is listed in `host_permissions`, which would sidestep the CORS problem; this is a design option, not a sourced finding (no source fetched on this point).
- crt.sh should be used only from a backend with caching or replaced by a commercial CT API; it is not viable per page load.
- Legit brand-new airdrop domains: the only safe treatment is "new domain + brand token + action word" = warn (not block) unless the host is in a curated allowlist of official claim domains that is updated from project announcements; weight social proof (Interisle: 77% of phishing domains are malicious registrations, so an unknown new domain has a high prior, but legitimate launches are a known FP class).

### Gaps
- No source directly measured hours-between-registration-and-first-phish for crypto drainer domains; the 8.6-day lifetime and 63%<4 days figures are general phishing.
- No live test was done of which registries (.xyz, .io, .top, .fi, .app) send CORS headers; the Dart-docs claim covers only IANA/Verisign/PIR/Google.
- Whether `data.iana.org` sends CORS headers for the bootstrap file is not confirmed by any source found.
- No source on crt.sh "Logged At" as a reliable first-seen proxy.

## Key Question 4: Typosquatting / homoglyph / combosquatting detection techniques and JS libraries

### Takeaway
MetaMask's production detector uses a tiny 8-entry fuzzylist with Levenshtein tolerance 1 on the hostname with www. and TLD stripped, and its maintainers say adding fuzzylist entries "almost always" causes more harm than good because of legitimate lookalikes; a "brand token inside hostname" approach combined with a public-suffix-aware registrable-domain parser (tldts) and a UTS#39 confusables skeleton is better evidenced than pure edit-distance.

### Cited Findings
- eth-phishing-detect algorithm (lists-ref.md): strip leading `www.`, strip the TLD (everything after the last dot, which mishandles `.co.uk`), compute Levenshtein distance to each fuzzylist entry, flag if distance <= tolerance; doc says default 3, configured 2 — [eth-phishing-detect lists-ref.md](https://github.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/blob/main/doc/lists-ref.md); the live config.json actually sets `tolerance: 1` and a fuzzylist of only 8 entries (cryptokitties.co, etherscan.io, launchpad.ethereum.org, makerfoundation.com, metamask.io, myetherwallet.com, opensea.io, satoshilabs.com) — [config.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/main/src/config.json) (the doc and the config disagree; the config is authoritative)
- Maintainers' policy: the fuzzylist "is a relic from a time where the ecosystem was smaller and the products had longer, more unique names"; "the risk of adding a website to the fuzzylist almost always outweighs the potential benefits"; any added entry requires quickly allowlisting its legitimate lookalikes, and repeated allowlisting leads to removal of the fuzzylist entry — [lists-ref.md](https://github.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/blob/main/doc/lists-ref.md); [MetaMask repo README](https://github.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect)
- Concrete legitimate lookalikes MetaMask had to allowlist (false positives of fuzzy matching): metamark.lt, metacask.com, metacask.io, metatask.io, metmask.com, cryptofitties.com, cryptotitties.fun, etcerscan.com, etherscan.com, opengea.org, opensea.gr — [config.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/main/src/config.json)
- dnstwist generates permutations (homoglyph with a curated Unicode range, hyphenation, dictionary words, TLD swap via `--tld dictionaries/common_tlds.dict`, plus addition/bitsquatting/insertion/omission/repetition/replacement/subdomain/transposition/vowel-swap listed in its fuzzer set), supports `--registered` filtering, IDN, rogue MX detection, JSON/CSV output, and ships a dictionary of "the most common words used in phishing campaigns" — [dnstwist GitHub](https://github.com/elceef/dnstwist)
- tldts: TypeScript Public Suffix List parser with continuously updated list, UMD/ESM/CJS builds, migration note that `{ allowPrivateDomains: true }` is needed to match psl's handling of private suffixes (github.io, blogspot.com, etc.) — [tldts GitHub](https://github.com/remusao/tldts); psl is the older library, also usable in browsers, with weaker maintenance scores — [psl package page](https://openapps.pro/packages/psl-npm)
- Private-suffix semantics example: registrable_domain("blogspot.com") is None; registrable_domain("foo.blogspot.com") is "foo.blogspot.com" — [psl2 docs](https://docs.rs/psl2)
- UTS #39 confusables in JS: `unicode-confusables` npm (isConfusing, per-char confusables, rectifyConfusion, detects zero-width chars; pinned to Unicode 10 confusables.txt, so dated) — [unicode-confusables README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/unicode-confusables@0.1.1/README.md); `ipfy/Homoglyph` multi-language domain-focused library targeting UTS#39 17.0.0 — [packagist](https://packagist.org/packages/ipfy/homoglyph)
- Chromium falls back to punycode display when a label mixes scripts, has a single non-ASCII char, or its skeleton matches a top domain (secondary description) — [unicodefyi guide](https://unicodefyi.com/guide/idn-homograph-detection/)
- IDN homographs are only 0.17% of phishing domains (Interisle) — so confusables checks have low recall but near-zero FP when restricted to mixed-script labels — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.29](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Combosquatting >> typosquatting (100x) in general phishing; most combosquats add one token — [giga.law](https://giga.law/blog/2017/12/13/combosquatting); Akamai 2022: combosquatting most common cybersquatting type in their phishing dataset — [Akamai](https://akamai.com/blog/security-research/combosquatting-keyword-analysis-support)

### Inferences
- Recommended pipeline: (1) `tldts.parse(hostname, {allowPrivateDomains:true})` to get registrable domain, subdomain and whether the host sits under a private suffix (pages.dev, vercel.app, github.io, webflow.io, gitbook.io); (2) exact allowlist/blocklist on both full hostname and registrable domain; (3) brand-token search: normalise (lowercase, strip hyphens/digits, UTS#39 skeleton) and test whether any allowlisted brand label appears as a substring/token in the hostname while the registrable domain is not that brand's; (4) edit-distance only for short, long-unique brand labels with tolerance 1 (MetaMask's current setting), never for short labels like "zora", "blur", "aave" where distance-1 neighbours are common English words or other brands.
- Score, don't block, on combosquat alone: Interisle shows 8.9% of phishing domains carry an exact brand and legitimate brand sprawl across TLDs is large (opensea.* x4, walletconnect.* x4).

### Gaps
- No published false-positive rate for MetaMask's fuzzylist, dnstwist or any confusables library.
- fast-levenshtein / string-similarity (JS) were not evaluated from sources; no claims made.
- Interisle did no near-match (typo) counting, so the share of crypto phishing that is typosquatting vs combosquatting is unknown; Inferno/Angel domain lists would answer this but were not accessible.

## Key Question 5: Hosting and infrastructure signals (Vercel/Netlify/GitHub Pages/IPFS/Cloudflare Pages/Fleek/Webflow, registrars, WHOIS privacy, nameservers, shared IP) and what an extension can check

### Takeaway
Free subdomain hosts (Cloudflare pages.dev/workers.dev +980%, Webflow, Vercel +279%, GitHub Pages, GitBook, IPFS gateways incl. Fleek) and Cloudflare fronting (53.6% of Angel Drainer domains, 26% of phishing NRDs, #1 hosting ASN) are heavily abused and the subdomain-provider signal is fully checkable in-extension from the hostname alone; registrar, nameserver, IP/ASN and shared-IP signals need RDAP/DNS/backend lookups and have weaker discriminative power because the same providers host huge legitimate populations.

### Cited Findings
- Interisle 2025: 13% of all reported phishing attacks used subdomain providers; 89% of those on just ten providers. 2025 ranking: #1 Cloudflare (pages.dev, workers.dev, trycloudflare.com, r2.dev) 33,418 attacks (+980% from 3,067), #2 Webflow (webflow.io) 30,703, #3 Google (web.app, firebaseapp.com, blogspot etc.), #4 Weebly, #5 GitBook (gitbook.io), #6 Vercel (vercel.app), #7 GitHub (github.io), #8 Duck DNS, #9 CentralNIC (uk.com etc.), #10 Glitch — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.21-22](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle Vercel case study: 279% increase in abuse of Vercel's free subdomain; Vercel auto-assigns `<project>.vercel.app` from the project name; hundreds of PancakeSwap-themed subdomains, plus `challenge-captcha-ten-10.vercel.app` style fake-security names — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.23](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle IPFS: 19,387 phishing sites on IPFS gateways in the period (up from 1,475 in 2022-23, then down 60% in the latest set); gateways abused: Protocol Labs/Fleek (flk-ipfs.xyz, fleek.co, fleek.cool: 8,743 then 4,642), Cloudflare (cf-ipfs.com, cloudflare-ipfs.com), dweb.link/ipfs.io, Infura (infura-ipfs.io: 4,351 then 2,036); example `ytjekcf7jwq4.ipfs.flk-ipfs.xyz`; Protocol Labs runs a "Bad Bits Denylist" — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.23-24](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle hosting ASNs: phishing seen in 4,065 networks; #1 Cloudflare AS13335 539,911 attacks (top for 4 years, reverse proxy hides origin), #2 Amazon AS16509, #3 Shenzhen Tencent, #4 Alibaba (US), #5 Fastly (edge IPs mask origin like Cloudflare) — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.19-20](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Interisle registrars (gTLD phishing domains, 2025): NICENIC 114,493 (score 8,192.4 on <150K domains), NameSilo 114,317 (4.5M domains), Dominet (HK) 96,650 (+477%, toll scams), NameCheap 83,587, Key-Systems 75,713 (74,737 .BOND); GoDaddy 66K on 65M; 5-year most-abused list: NameCheap, NameSilo, GoDaddy, PDR, Tucows, DNSpod, Dominet, Sav.com, Onamae, Key-Systems — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.25-26](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- UCL WEIS 2025: NameSilo most abused registrar for NRD phishing (offers free AI domain-name generation and accepts Bitcoin); only 2.1% at Namecheap; 26% of resolved domains behind Cloudflare — [UCL WEIS 2025 PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10209951/1/CDA_Domains___WEIS_25.pdf)
- Sucuri Angel Drainer: 5,751 unique domains in ~4 weeks, 53.6% CLOUDFLARENET — [Sucuri](https://blog.sucuri.net/2024/02/web3-crypto-malware-angel-drainer.html)
- Crypto drainer examples on free hosts (PhishDestroy / URL scanners): `liveclaim-lucky04.vercel.app` (Angel Drainer kit), `premium0peanseamint9.vercel.app`, `luckiestmint7.vercel.app`, `nft-aridrop-demo.vercel.app`, `help-metmask.typedream.app`, `metamaskbox.github.io`, `mmetamaskcriptosignin.webflow.io`, `scroll-airdrop.github.io`; scanners note free-host subdomains "inherit the reputation of the parent domain" — [PhishDestroy liveclaim-lucky04](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/liveclaim-lucky04.vercel.app/); [malwaretips scan](https://tools.malwaretips.com/url-scan/nft-aridrop-demo.vercel.app); [PhishDestroy help-metmask.typedream.app](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/help-metmask.typedream.app/)
- MetaMask blocklist is dense with `*.pages.dev`, `*.vercel.app`, `*.github.io` entries (claim-zama.pages.dev, claim-sniffer.pages.dev, claim-yolo/pigeon/asteroid.pages.dev, claiminvites01/04.vercel.app, mintclaimpage402/408.vercel.app, claimdogecoin20.github.io) — [config.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/main/src/config.json)
- Gname.com described by PhishDestroy as "a registrar known for accommodating high-risk domains" (vendor assessment) — [PhishDestroy](https://phishdestroy.io/domain/trmlabscoin.sbs)
- Blockaid (2024): weekly new malicious dapps tied to Inferno Drainer rose from ~800 to ~2,400/week; ~180,000 malicious entities detected per week; 71M attacks prevented in 2024 — [KuCoin news citing Blockaid](https://kucoin.com/news/flash/blockaid-s-dashboard-reveals-71-million-prevented-attacks-in-2024); [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/news/inferno-drainer-resumes-activity-amid-bull-market)
- Legitimate counter-examples: Netlify is absent from all abuse rankings found; Interisle's subdomain-provider list is dominated by providers that also host enormous legitimate populations (Google, Cloudflare, GitHub); many legitimate crypto frontends are deployed on Vercel/Cloudflare Pages/IPFS by design (Web3 "decentralised frontends" rationale described by Interisle) — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.23](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf) (no source found quantifying legitimate crypto dapps on vercel.app; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- Checkable in-extension at page load with zero network calls: hostname under a known free-subdomain suffix (pages.dev, workers.dev, trycloudflare.com, r2.dev, webflow.io, vercel.app, github.io, gitbook.io, web.app, firebaseapp.com, typedream.app, glitch.me, duckdns.org, *.ipfs.* gateway patterns incl. flk-ipfs.xyz, cf-ipfs.com, infura-ipfs.io, dweb.link, ipfs.io). Combined with a brand token or action word in the subdomain label this is a high-precision signal; alone it is weak because of legitimate preview deployments.
- Checkable with one RDAP call (if CORS allows): registrar name/IANA ID (NICENIC, NameSilo, Dominet, Gname, Key-Systems = elevated prior), registration date, nameservers (RDAP returns nameserver objects). WHOIS privacy/redaction is near-universal post-GDPR, so "privacy WHOIS" has essentially no discriminative value (inference; no source quantifies it).
- Requires backend: IP/ASN resolution (extension cannot do DNS lookups directly; `webRequest` exposes the connected IP only in some browsers), shared-IP/shared-infrastructure clustering (PhishDestroy's "cluster of ~8 domains sharing the same fingerprint"), passive DNS, CT monitoring. Cloudflare fronting is so common on both sides (Cloudflare is #1 ASN for phishing AND a mainstream CDN) that ASN alone should carry little weight.
- Custom domain + Cloudflare Pages/Vercel is indistinguishable from the hostname; only the free `*.pages.dev`/`*.vercel.app` form is detectable client-side.

### Gaps
- No report quantifies what fraction of legitimate crypto dapps/mint pages are served from vercel.app/pages.dev/IPFS subdomains, so the FP rate of the subdomain-provider signal for crypto specifically is unknown.
- No source on Netlify, Fleek-hosted legitimate frontends (e.g., Uniswap's IPFS releases) or Porkbun/Dynadot abuse rates for crypto phishing specifically; Dynadot and Gname appear only in market-share tables (UCL) and one vendor comment.
- Nameserver pattern evidence (e.g., Cloudflare NS vs registrar default NS) not found in any source.

## Key Question 6: Referrer/context signals (t.co/X, Discord, Telegram, Google Ads malvertising, QR) and search-ad phishing for crypto brands

### Takeaway
X/Twitter (fake or hijacked accounts and reply-comments) is the best-documented delivery channel for drainer sites, Google Search ads impersonating wallets (Phantom, MetaMask, PancakeSwap) are documented by Check Point, and Discord server compromises distributing fake mint links are documented by TRM; however no source gives a percentage breakdown of drainer traffic by channel, and official channels themselves get hijacked, so referrer should modulate thresholds rather than act as an allow or block signal.

### Cited Findings
- ScamSniffer mid-2024: victims were "primarily directed to phishing websites through fake comments on Twitter accounts"; the 2024 annual report says fake-account activity "largely mirrored phishing website trends, with a sharp drop in July tied to X's crackdown on fake accounts" — [TechFlow summary of ScamSniffer H1 2024](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/newsletter/52131); [ScamSniffer 2024 report](https://drops.scamsniffer.io/?p=971)
- ScamSniffer January 2024: ~11,000 phishing websites detected in that single month — [ScamSniffer Jan 2024](https://drops.scamsniffer.io/scamsniffer-report-over-55-million-stolen-due-to-crypto-phishing-in-january/)
- ScamSniffer 2025: non-drainer vectors listed include compromised social-media accounts posting phishing links, malicious frontend injections, npm supply-chain, fake verification pages and malicious Telegram bots — [ScamSniffer 2025](https://drops.scamsniffer.io/scam-sniffer-2025-crypto-phishing-losses-fall-83-to-84-million/)

- Chainbase fake-airdrop campaign distributed via malicious ads, social-media spam and DMs — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/fake-chainbase-airdrop-sites-drain-crypto-wallets-in-sophisticated-phishing-campaign/)
- TRM Labs: NFT-mint phishing via compromised Discord accounts rose 55% month-over-month in June 2022; ~$22M lost since May 2022; attacks were connected — [TRM Labs](https://trmlabs.com/resources/blog/trms-analysis-of-recent-surge-in-discord-hacks-shows-some-attacks-are-connected)

### Inferences
- `document.referrer` / `Referer` header on arrival from t.co, discord.com/discordapp.com, t.me, or a Google Ads click (`gclid` query param, `googleadservices.com`/`googlesyndication` referrer chain) is observable in-extension and should lower the warning threshold for brand+action hostnames that are not allowlisted; it should never raise trust (official channels are hijacked).
- QR-code arrivals have no referrer; treat "no referrer + new domain + wallet connect prompt" as equivalent to social arrival. No source found on QR-code crypto phishing specifically.

### Gaps
- No quantitative channel breakdown (X vs Discord vs Google Ads vs Telegram) for drainer traffic in any ScamSniffer/SlowMist/Chainabuse report found.
- Check Point's Google Ads report date was not confirmed; it may predate 2023.
- No 2023-2026 source found on QR-code-delivered crypto phishing.

## Key Question 7: Academic literature (2022-2026) on crypto/Web3 phishing website detection: feature sets, precision/recall, FP rates

### Takeaway
Most 2023-2025 academic Web3 phishing work measures on-chain behaviour (drainer contracts, DaaS profit sharing, NFT phishing) rather than classifying websites by URL/domain features; the one Web3-adjacent URL classifier found (GoPlus/PMANet) is URL-text-only (AUC 0.9941, no precision/recall/FPR reported), so there is no peer-reviewed false-positive rate for domain-layer heuristics specific to crypto phishing.

### Cited Findings
- IMC 2025 "A Deep Dive into Drainer-as-a-Service Phishing on Ethereum" (Zhejiang Univ. + MBZUAI; first author Bowen He): dataset built from on-chain profit-sharing; 32,819 phishing websites deployed with drainer toolkits; ~$135M from 76,582 victims (Mar 2023-Apr 2025); 1,910 profit-sharing contracts, 87,077 profit-sharing txs, 56 core operator accounts, 6,087 affiliates; only 10.8% of DaaS accounts labelled on Etherscan — [ACM DL (403 on fetch)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3730567.3764476); [BlockSec blog](https://blocksec.com/blog/inside-ethereum-s-shadow-economy-new-research-unmasks-the-135-m-drainer-as-a-service-industry)
- NDSS 2025 "Dissecting Payload-based Transaction Phishing on Ethereum" (Zhuo Chen et al.): uses VenomDrainer, InfernoDrainer, AngelDrainer as ground truth; transaction-payload focus, not domain-level — [NDSS 2025 PDF](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-311-paper.pdf)
- MBZUAI 2025 "Phishing Tactics Are Evolving: An Empirical Study of Phishing Contracts on Ethereum": 37,654 phishing contracts deployed 29 Dec 2022-1 Jan 2025 — [MBZUAI repository](https://irep.mbzuai.ac.ae/items/0b868ef9-d3b8-4617-b88e-ddccad586815)
- arXiv 2307.01579 "With Trail to Follow: Measurements of Real-world NFT Phishing Attacks on Ethereum" (2023) exists; content not fetched — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01579)
- arXiv 2512.15030 "ScamSweeper: Detecting Illegal Accounts in Web3 Scams via Transactions Analysis" (account-level, not URL) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.15030)
- GoPlus phishing-site detection whitepaper: URL-only approach because phishing pages are short-lived and content archives are hard to get; CharBERT post-trained backbone with hierarchical features, layer-aware attention and spatial pyramid pooling (= PMANet, arXiv 2311.12372, Liu et al., v2 Mar 2025); reports AUC 0.9941 and "near-perfect detection accuracy" but no precision/recall/FPR, no dataset size on the page — [GoPlus whitepaper](https://whitepaper.gopluslabs.io/goplus-network/research/phishing-site-detection); [arXiv 2311.12372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12372)
- General (non-crypto) phishing-URL literature: PhishWeb lexicographic domain-name analysis reports precision and recall >90% on open domain datasets — [AIT publication](https://publications.ait.ac.at/de/publications/phish-me-if-you-can-lexicographic-analysis-and-machine-learning-f/); a 2023 Applied Sciences study found URL+HTTP features outperform all-features — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/140b475961ce4c15b793574c885ca376); 2023 Symmetry paper uses LightGBM on domain-name features — [search result summary only]; arXiv 1510.06501 (2015, old) reports FPR 0.0005-0.001 when combining feature sets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.06501); benchmark study arXiv 2010.12847 (2020) lists domain age, WHOIS registration, registration length, DNS record checks as standard features — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12847)
- "A Large-Scale Analysis of Phishing Websites Hosted on Free Web Hosting Domains" (arXiv 2212.02563) exists and is relevant to the subdomain-provider signal; not fetched — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.02563)
- Agarwal & Vasek WEIS 2025 (newly registered phishing domains) is the best recent infrastructure-level measurement; see KQ3/KQ5 — [UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10209951)

### Inferences
- Because no crypto-specific paper reports FPR for domain-layer features, the extension should calibrate its own thresholds against (a) MetaMask's allowlist (58 entries, including 11 legitimate lookalikes) as a seed FP set, (b) the MetaMask blocklist as a positive set, and (c) a hand-collected list of official claim domains (KQ2) as the hardest FP set.
- URL-text deep models (PMANet) could run in a backend but their reported "near-perfect" numbers lack FPR, so they should not be the only gate.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2023-2026 paper found that classifies Web3 phishing websites using hostname/RDAP/CT/hosting features with reported precision/recall/FPR.
- IMC 2025 DaaS paper content (likely has TLD/hosting/naming stats of 32,819 sites) was inaccessible (403).
- arXiv 2307.01579 and 2212.02563 not read; they may contain domain-level statistics.

## Key Question 8: How to combine signals safely (synthesis of signal strength and FP risk)

### Takeaway
Only the exact blocklist and "free-subdomain host + brand token + action word" come close to block-worthy precision; every other domain-layer signal (TLD, age, registrar, Cloudflare, combosquat, referrer) has a documented legitimate population and should be additive score contributors gated by a curated allowlist of official domains, with "warn + explain" rather than "block" for scores in the middle band.

### Cited Findings
- Signal strength evidence summarised from the sources above: domain age (UCL 8.6-day lifetime; DNSRF 63%<4 days) — [UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10209951/1/CDA_Domains___WEIS_25.pdf), [DNSRF](https://dnsrf.org/blog/phishing-attacks--newly-registered-domains-still-a-prominent-threat); malicious-registration prior 77% (Interisle) — [Interisle press release](https://interisle.net/press/pl2025-press-release); subdomain-provider abuse growth (Cloudflare +980%, Vercel +279%) — [Interisle PDF](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf); brand-in-domain 8.9% of phishing domains — [Interisle PDF](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf); MetaMask's fuzzylist policy and allowlisted lookalikes — [lists-ref.md](https://github.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/blob/main/doc/lists-ref.md), [config.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect/main/src/config.json)
- Interisle notes risk-averse organisations "have resorted to blocklisting entire TLDs" and that high scores "erode the reputation of a TLD" so legitimate registrants avoid them — evidence that TLD-wide blocking is practised but has collateral cost — [Interisle 2025 PDF, p.13](https://domainnamewire.com/wp-content/Interisle_Phishing-Landscape-2025.pdf)
- Legitimate FP population documented: separate claim domains (eigenfoundation.org, zknation.io, layerzero.network), .xyz/.fi brands (sophon.xyz, ethena.fi), legitimate lookalikes (metacask.io, etherscan.com), hijacked official channels (Arbitrum, Blast) — see KQ2 citations

### Inferences
Suggested scoring (inference; weights are not from any source and must be tuned):
- Hard allow: hostname or registrable domain in curated allowlist (seeded from MetaMask whitelist + official claim domains). Allowlist must be host-specific for free-host providers (allow `foo.vercel.app`, never `vercel.app`).
- Hard block: exact match on MetaMask blocklist (or other feeds) by hostname or registrable domain.
- Strong (+3): registrable domain <7 days old (RDAP registration event, latest); free-subdomain host AND subdomain label contains brand token or action word; brand token inside hostname while registrable domain is not the brand's and not allowlisted.
- Medium (+2): TLD in {.top .bond .xin .cyou .sbs .cfd .icu .click .lol .monster .pics .shop .vip}; registrar in {NICENIC, Dominet, Gname, Key-Systems (for .bond), NameSilo}; action word (claim/airdrop/reward/mint/checker/eligib/alloc/presale/restake/points/v2/v3) in hostname of a non-allowlisted domain; arrival via t.co/discord/t.me/gclid.
- Weak (+1): .xyz TLD; domain 7-30 days old; Cloudflare/Fastly fronting; mixed-script or confusable label (rare but high precision when present); digits appended to brand/action token.
- Negative: domain >1 year old (-2), domain in Tranco/top-list or allowlist (-3), hostname is a subdomain of an allowlisted registrable domain (-3, but keep +signals for free hosts).
- Bands: >=5 block-with-override; 3-4 interstitial warning with the reasons listed; <=2 passive badge. Any wallet-connect/signature request on a 3-4 page should escalate to the warning.
- Latency plan: all hostname/TLD/action-word/subdomain-provider/referrer checks are synchronous and local (<1 ms). RDAP is one cached fetch (rdap.org 302 + registry; 10 req/10 s limit; CORS confirmed only for Verisign/PIR/Google/IANA) and should be fired in the background with the badge updated when it returns; crt.sh and IP/ASN/shared-infra lookups go to an optional backend.

### Gaps
- No source provides validated weights or a measured precision/recall for any combined scheme on crypto phishing; the above is a design proposal to be validated on the MetaMask block/allow lists plus a hand-built official-claim-domain set.
